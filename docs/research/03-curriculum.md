# Research 3: Highest-value curriculum (the topics with the biggest payoff)

Sources: the JustinGuitar Practical Music Theory course and community, Leavitt (Berklee), Edwards (*Fretboard Logic*), Tagliarino (*Fretboard Workbook*), Tomo Fujita (*Guitar Wisdom*), Ted Greene, and teacher blogs (links at the end).

**Where the sources agree:**

- Learn the notes on strings 6 and 5, plus octave shapes, first.
- Then intervals, then triads.
- Then CAGED as the map that connects them.
- Then the harmonised major scale and Roman numerals. These unlock progressions, transposition and capo use.
- **Triads are the single highest-leverage intermediate topic.** Once you know them, CAGED "comes for free".
- Modes are low priority.

## Curriculum map

Every topic ID below is used throughout the design (`docs/design/`).

### Tier 0: Foundations

| ID | Topic | Learner can DO (quizzable) |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Musical alphabet, half and whole steps, sharps and flats, B–C and E–F | Name the note ±1 or 2 semitones away in under 2s; spell the chromatic scale |
| F2 | Natural notes on strings 6 and 5, frets 0–12 | Name any fret in under 2s; find any note in under 3s |
| F3 | Fret-marker landmarks (3/5/7/9/12) and the 12th-fret repeat | Orient from a marker |
| F4 | Rhythm basics: pulse, subdivision, counting | Tap within ±30ms of a click |

### Tier 1: Core

| ID | Topic | Learner can DO |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Octave shapes, including the G–B shift | Tap every octave of a note in a region; find all Gs in under 15s |
| C2 | All notes on all strings, including accidentals | Name a random fret in under 2s at ≥90% accuracy |
| C3 | Intervals as shapes (m2–P8), including the G–B +1 fret shift | Tap the M3/P5/b7 from a root; name the interval between two dots |
| C4 | Major scale construction and degrees 1–7 | Spell any major key; "the 6th of E♭?" |
| C5 | Chord construction: triads, then maj7/7/m7/m7b5, sus, add9 | Spell Bm7; find the 3rd or b7 in a shape |
| C6 | CAGED: 5 shapes, roots, chaining C→A→G→E→D | Place a shape at a root; name the next shape; play one chord in 5 positions |
| C7 | Minor and major pentatonic boxes linked to CAGED | Link each box to its shape; find the relative minor or major |
| C8 | Diatonic harmony: I ii iii IV V vi vii°, Roman numerals, Nashville numbers | Chords in any key; numeral ↔ chord |
| C9 | Common progressions: I–IV–V, I–V–vi–IV, vi–IV–I–V, I–vi–IV–V, ii–V–I, 12-bar blues (including the quick change), i–bVII–bVI–bVII | Play in any key; recognise from a chart and by ear |
| C10 | Capo and transposition | "Song in B♭, using G shapes: which capo fret?" |

### Tier 2: Intermediate

| ID | Topic | Learner can DO |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | **Triads on string sets** (1–3, 2–4, 3–5, 4–6), all inversions | All 3 inversions on each string set; voice-lead I–IV–V |
| I2 | CAGED arpeggios and chord-scale relationships | Chord tones inside any box |
| I3 | Keys, key signatures, circle of fifths, relative minor | Count sharps and flats; give the relative minor and the V |
| I4 | Minor-key harmony, harmonic-minor V, iiø–V7–i | Explain E7 in A minor |
| I5 | Chord function and substitution: relative, secondary dominants, borrowed chords, tritone | Classify a chord's function; spot the borrowed chord |
| I6 | Ear training for intervals, chord quality, bass movement and progression ID (runs from day one) | Hear I–V–vi–IV vs vi–IV–I–V |
| I7 | Major scale in CAGED or 3NPS positions | Play 5 positions; start from any degree |

### Tier 3: Advanced (optional, lower payoff)

- Modes as chord-scale colours
- Drop-2 and drop-3 voicings, shell voicings, extensions
- Advanced reharmonisation
- Harmonic and melodic minor
- Advanced rhythm

## Recommended order

1. F1–F3.
2. C1–C2 together with C3.
3. C4–C5.
4. C8–C10. Start these **early, in parallel with the fretboard work**, because songs drive motivation.
5. I6 ear drills, starting on day one.
6. C6–C7.
7. I1–I2.
8. I3–I5, then I7.
9. Advanced topics, as optional modules.

### Mic limitation

Pitch alone doesn't tell you which string a note was played on: C4 at string 3 fret 5 sounds the same as string 2 fret 1. So a mic drill should do one of these:

- accept any position that produces the right pitch,
- constrain the drill to a region of the neck so only one position fits,
- check only the pitch class.

Single notes are detected reliably, apart from octave errors. Strummed chords can only be judged by chroma (root and quality), not by voicing.

## Common pain points and misconceptions (what feedback should target)

- **F1:** no sharp between B–C or E–F; enharmonic spelling.
- **F2/C2:** counting up from the nut instead of recalling; plateauing because frets 7–12 on the middle strings get neglected. The app should target the weak cells.
- **C1/C3:** the G–B shift breaks octave and interval shapes. Teach it explicitly and drill it interleaved.
- **C5:** confusing the b7 in "7" with the 7 in "maj7"; misreading "m7".
- **C6:** treating CAGED as 5 grips to memorise rather than a map; thinking it replaces note knowledge; full G and C shapes are hard to fret, so **start from partial shapes and triads**.
- **C7:** "box prison"; not hearing the root.
- **C9:** not realising that I–V–vi–IV and vi–IV–I–V are rotations of each other.
- **C10:** confusing the chord shape name with the sounding pitch; transposing in the wrong direction.
- **I1:** too many shapes with no system. Show that triads are the cores of the CAGED shapes.
- **I4:** "E7 isn't in A minor".
- **Modes:** taught as "the scale from the 2nd degree" without harmonic context.
- **F4:** theory-heavy learners neglect timing.
